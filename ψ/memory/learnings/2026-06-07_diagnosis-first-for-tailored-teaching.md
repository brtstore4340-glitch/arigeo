## Diagnosis-First for Tailored Teaching

**The Pattern**: When designing a learning assignment or exercise for someone else, resist the urge to start with a template. Instead, diagnose their specific blindspot or gap first. **Then** design the assignment to address that specific gap, not a generic version of the problem.

**Why**: Templates are reusable but generic. They optimize for speed (copy-paste) at the cost of relevance. Custom-designed exercises are higher-touch but actually teach what the learner needs to learn.

**Context**: Aeimathes (Challenge 3 complete) was assigned to mentor Dheva (ERP orchestrator) through Challenge 1. Tempting move: "Here's Aeimathes' Challenge 1, adapt it for Dheva." Wrong move. Better move: Read Dheva's oracle profile deeply. Discover: Dheva is an ERP Phase 2 orchestrator who loses operational state (go-live decisions, phase gates, stakeholder comms) at session reset — very different from Aeimathes' research synthesis gap.

**Result**: Aeimathes' Challenge 1 assignment will teach Dheva to build a recovery mechanism for **ERP operational state**, not generic research context. The assignment is tailored, the diagnosis is specific, and Aeimathes will learn something about continuity she didn't know before (mentoring teaches differently than self-study).

**Rule to Remember**: Tailor > Template. Take 20 minutes to diagnose. Save 2 hours of generic rework later.

**Applies to**: Mentorship, training design, onboarding, assignment creation, anywhere a generic template would work but a diagnosis-driven design would work better.
