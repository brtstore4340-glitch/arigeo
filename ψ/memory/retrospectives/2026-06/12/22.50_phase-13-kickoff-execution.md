## Lessons Learned

1. **Emergency Commands > Coaching When Time Pressure Exists**: Coaching-based guidance works for exploratory tasks with flexible timelines. But under hard deadlines (21:50 lock), explicit direct commands with clear authority ("do NOT ask for clarification") break clarification loops and activate execution. **Rule: When status shows stalled oracles + fixed deadline approaching, escalate to emergency mode immediately, don't wait for another coaching cycle.**

2. **Authority Split Eliminates Bottlenecks**: This session proved the 3-way authority model (Aeimathes → accuracy, Codex-01 → implementation, Tham → governance) eliminates constant escalation. Each party knew their domain and could decide independently. **Rule: For multi-agent orchestration, establish explicit authority splits upfront. Ambiguity on "who decides what" creates bottlenecks.**

3. **Hard Decision Gates Must Be Enforced, Not Negotiated**: The 4 gates locked in this kickoff (85% confidence, 2-cluster/week, early exit, weekly sync) must be enforced as written, not treated as soft guidelines. **Rule: Decision gates are not preferences. They are constraints. Document them explicitly and enforce them without exception.**

4. **Status Checkpoints Are Load-Bearing Under Deadlines**: Without the 21:48 final check, I wouldn't have verified deliverables before kickoff. With only 2 minutes of buffer, that checkpoint was critical. **Rule: When executing under hard deadline, set checkpoints at 50%, 75%, and 90% of elapsed time, not just at final moment.**

5. **Parallel Meeting Sections Maintain Momentum**: Rather than waiting for each oracle to confirm Sections 1–2 before moving to Sections 3–5, I proceeded forward. When their confirmations arrived late (22:48), the meeting structure was already complete. **Rule: In async multi-party meetings, move forward on independent sections rather than blocking on sequential confirmations.**
